Medicare Part D 2027: The $2,400 Cap, $700 Deductible, and What Your ANOC Really Says Before AEP Opens Oct 15

By HealthCalc Team

Published October 12, 2026

7 min read

AEP opens in 3 days. The Annual Election Period for the 2027 plan year runs October 15, 2026 through December 7, 2026. Any plan change you make during AEP takes effect January 1, 2027. Your Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) should have arrived by September 30, 2026 — if yours didn’t, call your plan now.

Three Part D numbers change on January 1, 2027, and two of them are quietly bigger stories than they look. The out-of-pocket cap goes from $2,100 to $2,400. The maximum allowable annual deductible rises from $615 to $700. And the national average stand-alone prescription drug plan (PDP) premium is projected at about $36 per month, up from $35.09 in 2026.

The headline numbers were finalized by CMS in its April 2026 Rate Announcement and confirmed when the 2027 Landscape Source File was posted on September 28, 2026. 2027 Star Ratings followed on October 8. ANOCs mailed to current Part D and Medicare Advantage members by the September 30 statutory deadline carry the plan-level specifics. This is the week to read yours.

Here is what actually changes, what stays the same, and the five lines on your ANOC that decide whether you should switch plans before December 7.

The 2027 Part D Numbers at a Glance

Benefit Parameter 2026 2027 Change
Annual out-of-pocket cap $2,100 $2,400 +$300
Max annual deductible $615 $700 +$85
Avg stand-alone PDP premium (projected) $35.09 ~$36.00 +~$0.91
Avg MA plan premium (projected) $4.47 $2.81 −$1.66
Insulin out-of-pocket cap (per month) $35 $35 No change
Donut hole / coverage gap Eliminated Eliminated Unchanged since 2025
What the $2,400 cap means in plain English: Once you have paid $2,400 out of pocket on covered Part D drugs in calendar year 2027 (deductible plus copays and coinsurance — not monthly premiums), you pay $0 for covered prescriptions for the rest of the year. The cap is per person, not per household, and it resets on January 1, 2028.

Why the Cap Went Up (and Why That’s Not a Rollback)

The $2,000 cap enacted by the Inflation Reduction Act for 2025 was not a static ceiling. The statute indexes it each year to the growth rate of per-capita Part D costs. It rose to $2,100 in 2026 and now to $2,400 in 2027. The protection itself — the elimination of the donut hole, the hard annual ceiling, the catastrophic phase at $0 — is permanent. Only the dollar figure moves.

For most beneficiaries the practical effect of the $300 increase is modest. If you reach the cap in a typical year, you are reaching it on expensive specialty drugs where the extra $300 shows up as roughly one additional month of coinsurance before you hit catastrophic coverage.

The Part D Premium Stabilization Demonstration Ends December 31

This is the quietly bigger story. CMS’s Part D Premium Stabilization Demonstration — the temporary federal subsidy that cushioned plan-level premium swings in 2025 and 2026 — expires December 31, 2026.

The national average PDP premium barely moves ($35.09 to roughly $36), but individual plans don’t pay national averages. Some plans will reset to the full premium without the stabilization subsidy underneath them, and beneficiaries in those plans may see monthly premium changes well above or below the national average. The number to read is the "Monthly premium" row on page 1 or 2 of your ANOC, compared against what you’re paying now.

If your ANOC shows a sharp premium increase for 2027: that is usually not a mistake. The combination of the stabilization demonstration ending and standard reinsurance recalculations means some plans are repricing more than they did last year. The right response is to run Medicare Plan Finder drug-by-drug, not to assume the plan became worse overall. A plan with a higher premium but better formulary tiering for your drugs can still win on total cost.

The Second Group of IRA-Negotiated Drugs Takes Effect January 1, 2027

The first group of 10 Medicare-negotiated prices (Eliquis, Jardiance, Xarelto, Januvia, Farxiga, Entresto, Enbrel, Imbruvica, Stelara, insulin aspart) continues at their 2026 Maximum Fair Prices in 2027. The second group of 15 selected drugs begins its negotiated prices on January 1, 2027.

Your plan’s 2027 formulary will show whether each of these medications remains covered and at what tier. The negotiated price doesn’t automatically flow through to a lower copay — it determines what Medicare and the plan jointly pay before your coinsurance kicks in. Check the tier placement, not just the list.

Five Lines to Read on Your ANOC

Your Annual Notice of Change is the one document that tells you how your plan is repricing, not what the national average is doing. Five lines matter more than the rest:

  1. Monthly premium. Compare 2027 against what you pay now. Flag anything moving more than $5–10/month for closer scrutiny.
  2. Annual deductible. Many plans will shift toward the new $700 max. If your plan previously had a $0 deductible and now shows one, that is a meaningful change — it changes when you start paying copays, not just how much.
  3. Formulary tier for each drug you take. A medication moving from Tier 2 to Tier 3 (or Tier 3 to Tier 4) can quietly add $20–$80 per month to your out-of-pocket cost.
  4. Preferred pharmacies list. If your current pharmacy drops out of the preferred network for 2027, your copays rise even with no other change.
  5. Mail-order and 90-day supply options. Changes here can save (or cost) hundreds per year on maintenance medications.

Everything else — letter formatting, Star Rating changes, administrative language — is secondary.

What Isn’t Changing in 2027

  • The three-phase structure (deductible → initial coverage → catastrophic). No donut hole.
  • The $35/month insulin cap under Part D.
  • Vaccines on the CDC adult immunization schedule are still $0 cost-share.
  • The Medicare Prescription Payment Plan (M3P) continues — you can still spread your out-of-pocket costs across the plan year in level monthly installments. Enrollment is per plan year, and the opt-in paths are cleaner than they were in 2025.
  • Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy) eligibility and benefits.

The 2027 Medicare Advantage Landscape in One Paragraph

CMS’s September 28 landscape file shows the average Medicare beneficiary can choose from 28 stand-alone PDPs in 2027, down from 32 in 2026, as several national carriers exited specific markets. Medicare Advantage plan counts also fell in many counties. Fewer plans doesn’t automatically mean a worse year for consumers — the remaining plans tend to be the larger, more established ones — but it does mean that running Plan Finder in October is more useful than ever, because a plan that was best for you in 2026 may simply not exist in 2027.

What to Do This Week (Before AEP Opens Oct 15)

Monday–Tuesday: Find your ANOC. If it never arrived, call your plan’s member services line and ask for a replacement — plans are required to send one.

Wednesday: Read the five lines above. Write your 2026 and 2027 numbers side-by-side on a single sheet of paper. Note any drug that moved tier.

October 15 (AEP opens): Run Medicare Plan Finder on medicare.gov with your current drug list. The tool now pulls 2027 formularies live. Even if your plan looks unchanged, check two or three alternatives in your ZIP code — the ending of premium stabilization means the right-plan ranking may have shuffled.

By December 7: If you’re switching, enroll by December 7. If you’re staying, no action needed — your plan auto-renews for 2027.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the $2,400 cap include my monthly premium?

No. The cap counts only the money you spend on covered drugs — deductible, copays, and coinsurance. Monthly premiums are separate and don’t count toward the cap.

What if I switch Part D plans mid-year in 2027?

Your out-of-pocket spending toward the cap transfers with you. If you’ve spent $1,800 under Plan A and switch to Plan B, you only need to spend $600 more under Plan B before reaching the $2,400 cap and $0 copays for the rest of 2027.

Is the $2,400 cap per person or per couple?

Per person. Each Medicare beneficiary has their own cap. Spouses both on Part D each have a separate $2,400 limit in 2027.

What happens to the first 10 negotiated drug prices in 2027?

They continue. The 2026 Maximum Fair Prices for Eliquis, Jardiance, Xarelto, Januvia, Farxiga, Entresto, Enbrel, Imbruvica, Stelara, and insulin aspart stay in force while the second group of 15 drugs begins its own negotiated prices on January 1, 2027.

How do I use the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan in 2027?

Opt in through your Part D plan at any time during the plan year. Your plan calculates your estimated 2027 out-of-pocket costs and bills you in level monthly installments instead of large pharmacy-counter payments. The total amount you pay doesn’t change — only the timing does. Most useful if an expensive specialty drug would hit you with a $500+ bill in January.
