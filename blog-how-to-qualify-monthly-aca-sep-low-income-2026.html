How to Qualify for the Monthly ACA Special Enrollment Period at Low Income in 2026: The 150% FPL Rule, Documentation Required, and Why Most People Who Qualify Never Use It

By HealthCalc Team

Published October 10, 2026

10 min read

Most people believe you can only sign up for a Marketplace plan during Open Enrollment or after a qualifying life event like a job loss, marriage, or move. That is true for the majority of applicants — but there is a permanent, year-round enrollment window that most people who qualify for it never find out exists. It applies to households whose projected annual income lands at or below 150% of the Federal Poverty Level, and in 2026 it covers a surprisingly wide slice of the country: individuals earning up to $23,475 a year and families of four earning up to $48,225.

If that describes your household, you can enroll in a Marketplace plan this week, next week, or in February — without waiting for Open Enrollment to roll around and without needing to document a life event. This is the walkthrough: who qualifies, exactly what to upload, how coverage effective dates work, and the three documentation mistakes that cause the majority of avoidable denials.

What the Monthly Low-Income SEP Actually Is

The monthly low-income Special Enrollment Period was adopted in 2022 and made permanent through subsequent Marketplace rules. It lets any household whose projected annual Modified Adjusted Gross Income is at or below 150% FPL enroll in a Marketplace plan any month of the year with advance premium tax credits. It is available on healthcare.gov in the 32 states that use the federal Marketplace and in nearly every state-based Marketplace that has adopted it (a handful have slightly different variations — California, New York, and Washington each run their own flavor).

Unlike a life-event SEP, you are not required to document that something happened. You only have to document that your projected 2026 income falls at or below the threshold.

The 2026 Income Thresholds, By Household Size

The 2026 Federal Poverty Level starts at a base of $15,650 for a household of one in the 48 contiguous states and DC, and increases by $5,580 for each additional person. The monthly SEP uses 150% of those numbers.

Important: the Marketplace uses projected 2026 annual income, not current month earnings. If you earned more in January and February and then lost shifts in September, the question is whether your full-year 2026 total will land at or below the ceiling — not what any single month looked like.

Who Qualifies: The Six-Part Test

To use the monthly low-income SEP you have to satisfy all six of the following at the moment you apply:

  1. Projected 2026 household MAGI at or below 150% FPL for your household size.
  2. Lawfully present in the United States and generally eligible to buy Marketplace coverage.
  3. Not enrolled in Medicare — Part A, Part B, or Advantage.
  4. Not enrolled in Medicaid or CHIP. A pending Medicaid application is fine; so is a Medicaid denial.
  5. Not eligible for affordable employer coverage under the family glitch fix rule — which asks whether the employer offer costs more than 8.39% of household income in 2026 for the applicant and for family members, calculated separately.
  6. Not already enrolled in another Marketplace plan with APTC. If you are, the existing plan has to be terminated first.

Residents of the ten states that still have a Medicaid coverage gap in 2026 whose income lands between the state Medicaid limit and 100% FPL often fall through the cracks: too high for Medicaid, too low for Marketplace subsidies. Those applicants should still submit an application. The Marketplace will issue a formal determination, which preserves the right to appeal and documents the gap for any future state program expansion.

What Documentation the Marketplace Actually Accepts

The Marketplace rarely asks for a thick packet. The documents it accepts to prove projected income are:

  • A recent pay stub showing hours and gross pay.
  • A signed self-employment income statement listing gross receipts and allowable expenses.
  • A Social Security Administration benefit award letter.
  • An unemployment benefits letter or determination.
  • A prior-year federal tax return (used as corroborating evidence, not as the sole proof).
  • Bank statements where income deposits are visible.
  • A signed self-attestation of projected income, when no other documentation exists.

Many applications are approved on an initial self-attestation and then the Marketplace follows up with a request for documentation within 90 days. If you miss that follow-up, the SEP can be rescinded and coverage ended retroactively, so set a calendar reminder when you first apply.

Documentation shortcut: If you were recently denied Medicaid, the denial notice itself is treated as strong supporting evidence that your projected income falls within the Marketplace SEP window. Keep a scan or photo of the denial letter and upload it to the application; it is often the single most persuasive document reviewers see.

Step-By-Step: The 20-Minute Application

From logging into healthcare.gov to confirmed enrollment, the process typically takes about 20 minutes for a single applicant and 30-40 minutes for a family. The sequence:

  1. Log in or create an account at healthcare.gov (or your state-based Marketplace). If you have applied before, use the same account so the system pulls prior data forward.
  2. Start a new application for 2026 coverage. The system will ask whether you are experiencing a qualifying life event; answer "no."
  3. Enter projected 2026 household income. Use annualized numbers, not monthly. Add income from all household tax members. If your situation changed mid-year (reduced hours, lost a side gig), explain it in the comment field.
  4. Confirm household size. Follow the tax household rule: you, your spouse if filing jointly, and anyone you will claim as a tax dependent.
  5. Answer the employer-coverage questions carefully. If anyone in the household has access to an employer offer, calculate 8.39% of household income and compare it to the employee cost of self-only coverage, and separately to the family cost under the family glitch fix.
  6. Receive the eligibility determination. If income is at or below 150% FPL and all other tests pass, the determination screen will offer the monthly low-income SEP and show the subsidy amount.
  7. Compare plans and enroll. At 150% FPL and below, a benchmark Silver plan typically has a $0 premium after APTC and comes with Cost-Sharing Reductions that cut deductibles and copays substantially.
  8. Pay the first premium. Even a $0 premium plan requires that you confirm enrollment with the insurer; some carriers send a one-dollar confirmation invoice. Coverage is not active until the carrier marks it so.

When Coverage Starts

Monthly low-income SEP enrollments follow the general Marketplace prospective-coverage rules:

Date you select a plan and pay Standard effective date
1st–15th of the month1st of the following month
16th–end of month1st of the second following month (varies by Marketplace)

If you have an urgent medical need, call the Marketplace Call Center (1-800-318-2596 for healthcare.gov) and ask whether a hardship effective-date exception is available. These are case-by-case but not uncommon for applicants with new diagnoses or scheduled procedures.

The Three Mistakes That Cause Most Avoidable Denials

  1. Reported income contradicts tax records without an explanation. If the Marketplace sees a prior-year AGI of $42,000 and a projected 2026 MAGI of $22,000 and no comment explaining the change, it will often ask for additional documentation and may suspend the SEP while waiting. Add one sentence to the comment field: "Hours cut in July; business is seasonal; left higher-earning job in April."
  2. Unresolved employer-coverage flags in the data hub. The federal data hub sometimes still shows an applicant as having access to employer coverage from a prior job. If the application won't advance past the employer-coverage screen, upload a short signed statement that the offer is no longer available or no longer affordable, and attach a termination letter or payroll change document if you have one.
  3. Already enrolled in another Marketplace plan. If you tried to enroll earlier in the year and never cancelled, the second application will stall. Call the Call Center to terminate the first enrollment, then restart the new application; the Call Center can usually coordinate the termination and the new SEP in the same call.

If Your Income Rises Mid-Year

You are allowed to enroll under the monthly low-income SEP based on projected income at the time of application. If your projected income rises later in the year, the SEP is not revoked retroactively, but you are required to report the change within 30 days through your Marketplace account. The Marketplace will recalculate your APTC going forward and reconcile the full year at tax time on Form 8962. Projected-income increases that remain under 150% FPL change nothing. Increases that cross the threshold change your subsidy amount but not your ongoing enrollment.

Why Most People Who Qualify Never Use It

CMS data from the first three years of the monthly SEP suggests that uptake has grown but still represents only a fraction of the people who are eligible. Three reasons dominate the gap:

  • Awareness. The SEP wasn't marketed on television the way Open Enrollment is; it was added quietly through a Marketplace rulemaking.
  • Perception. Many eligible households assume they are either "too poor" (expecting Medicaid to apply) or "not poor enough" (expecting the Marketplace to be for middle-income families) and don't check.
  • Prior denial memory. Applicants who were denied Marketplace coverage in earlier years when their circumstances or the law were different never reapplied once the rules changed.

If any of those describe someone in your household, or someone you help with their coverage, the monthly SEP is a 20-minute conversation that often ends with a $0-premium Silver plan and immediate access to a doctor's office.

The Short Version

Any month of the year, if your projected 2026 household income is at or below 150% FPL — $23,475 for one person, $48,225 for a family of four — you can enroll in a Marketplace plan with full subsidies and Cost-Sharing Reductions, without needing a qualifying life event. Log into healthcare.gov or your state Marketplace, start a new 2026 application, enter projected annual income, confirm you are not covered elsewhere, upload a pay stub or Medicaid denial notice if asked, and pick a Silver plan. Coverage usually starts the first of the following month. The three most common denials come from unexplained income gaps, old employer-coverage flags, and prior Marketplace enrollments that weren't terminated — all fixable with one phone call to the Call Center at 1-800-318-2596.
