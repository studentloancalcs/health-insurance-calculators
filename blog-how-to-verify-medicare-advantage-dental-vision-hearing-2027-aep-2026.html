How to Verify Your 2027 Medicare Advantage Dental, Vision, and Hearing Benefits Before AEP 2026 Ends

By HealthCalc Team

Published October 22, 2026

10 min read

The headline on your 2027 Medicare Advantage plan is probably going to look great. CMS is projecting the average Medicare Advantage premium will fall 16.5% from 2026 to 2027. Many plans will keep their $0 premium and will advertise it loudly through the AEP mailings and TV spots that are already in heavy rotation. What is quieter — and much more consequential for most enrollees — is what is happening to the dental, vision, and hearing benefits that sold those plans in the first place.

In the 2026 HealthScape Advisors survey of Medicare Advantage plan leaders, nearly 70% said their 2027 benefits would be less rich than 2026. Industry analysts covering the 2027 bid filings have reached the same conclusion: carriers are preserving the $0 premium headline and funding it in part by trimming the supplemental benefit categories that enrollees use less visibly. UnitedHealthcare and Humana are cutting plan counts; Centene is dropping 20% of its county footprint; Molina is exiting individual Medicare Advantage entirely. The plans that survive are, on average, offering lighter supplemental coverage for 2027.

For enrollees who actively use dental, vision, or hearing benefits, that distinction is not academic. A $500 drop in the annual dental maximum means a 2027 crown costs about $500 more out-of-pocket. A tightened hearing aid formulary means the Bluetooth-enabled rechargeable model you wanted may no longer be in the covered device list. A flex card that worked at the grocery self-checkout in 2026 may decline the same items in 2027. The only way to catch any of this before December 7 is to read the Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) and the 2027 Evidence of Coverage line by line. This guide walks through the five-minute verification workflow.

Why Carriers Are Trimming DVH Benefits for 2027

Three forces are compressing Medicare Advantage benefit designs. First, risk-adjustment and star-rating payment changes have reduced per-member revenue for many plans. Second, CMS's narrower interpretation of allowable Special Supplemental Benefits for the Chronically Ill (SSBCI) has pulled back on what grocery and OTC flex-card dollars can be used for and under what documentation. Third, utilization of dental, vision, and hearing benefits has risen as plans aged into their marketing cycles — and higher utilization has made these benefits more expensive for carriers to maintain.

The result is a textbook actuarial response: carriers protect the two features most sensitive to enrollment competition (the $0 premium and the Part B giveback where offered) and absorb the margin squeeze in the categories that are less visible on medicare.gov's Plan Finder summary. According to industry analysis of 2027 bid filings, the four supplemental benefits most frequently reduced are:

  • Comprehensive dental maximums, dropping on average $200 to $500 per year on many plans.
  • Over-the-counter and grocery flex-card dollars, both the quarterly allowance and the covered-item list.
  • Hearing aid allowances, with tighter device formularies and higher upgrade copays.
  • Vision allowances for frames and lenses, often converted from an open allowance to a vendor-specific allowance.

None of these cuts creates a Special Enrollment Period on its own. The ANOC is considered advance notice. If you want 2027 to look different, you have to act during the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15, 2026 through December 7, 2026).

Step 1 — Find the Three Documents That Control

Marketing materials, brochures, and TV ads are not contracts. Three documents are:

  1. The Annual Notice of Change (ANOC). Your plan was required to mail this to you by September 30, 2026. It presents a side-by-side comparison of each benefit's 2026 and 2027 values in a federally mandated format. The ANOC is where you will see what is actually changing.
  2. The 2027 Evidence of Coverage (EOC). This is the full contract. It describes every covered service, every exclusion, every prior authorization requirement, waiting periods, in-network and out-of-network cost sharing, and the specific vendors and device formularies that apply. The EOC controls when the ANOC and the marketing materials disagree.
  3. The medicare.gov Plan Finder. For plans you are not currently enrolled in, the Plan Finder gives a standardized summary of the 2027 benefits. It will not have the detail of the EOC, but it is the right starting point for comparison shopping.

If you cannot find your ANOC, call the plan's member services line and ask them to resend it in writing or direct you to it in the member portal. Plans are required to maintain it; the delay to retrieve a mailed copy is usually 7 to 10 business days, which is tight relative to the December 7 deadline but still workable.

Step 2 — Read the ANOC Dental Row

Open the ANOC to the supplemental benefits section and find the comprehensive dental row. In a well-formatted ANOC, the row will have a 2026 column, a 2027 column, and a short "What's Changing" note. Read all three carefully.

The specific numbers to compare:

Dental Benefit AttributeWhat to Check in 2026 vs. 2027
Annual comprehensive dental maximumAny drop of $100 or more is a benefit cut; shop other plans.
Preventive services coinsuranceShould be 100% both years. If 2027 shifts to 90% or 80%, cleanings cost $30-$60 more.
Basic services coinsurance (fillings, simple extractions)Shift from 100% to 50% is common; watch for the move.
Major services coinsurance (crowns, bridges, dentures)Typically 50% in both years; some plans drop major services entirely for 2027.
Waiting period for major services6 to 12 months in 2026 for new enrollees; some plans extend to 12 to 18 for 2027.
Dental networkCheck whether the plan uses a closed network or allows out-of-network. Closed-network plans with reduced provider panels are more common for 2027.
Implant coverageRare in 2026; even rarer in 2027. Confirm separately.

If any of these moved in a direction you do not like, flag the plan and move to Step 3.

Step 3 — Read the Vision and Hearing Rows

Vision

Vision benefits are typically smaller dollar amounts but affect the frames and lenses line most seniors use once a year. In the ANOC, compare:

  • Annual eye exam. Usually $0 copay in-network both years; confirm.
  • Frame allowance. Many plans are converting from a flexible $150 to $300 allowance to a vendor-specific allowance usable only at a designated optical chain (e.g., EyeMed, Davis Vision, UnitedHealthcare Vision). Verify the vendor and the dollar amount.
  • Lens coverage. Standard single-vision, bifocal, and trifocal lenses are often covered at $0 or a low copay. Progressives and anti-reflective coatings are typically add-on costs that are rising for 2027.
  • Contact lens allowance. If you wear contacts, check the specific annual allowance and whether it is combined with the frame allowance (it usually is).

Hearing

Hearing is where the quiet cuts are most consequential, because the retail cost of a mid-tier hearing aid pair is $4,000 to $6,000 and a Medicare Advantage plan's allowance is one of the few paths most retirees have to covered hearing aid financing.

  • Hearing exam. Should be $0 or low copay in-network, both years.
  • Hearing aid allowance. Compare the 2026 and 2027 columns. A drop from $2,000 to $1,500 per aid every three years is a $1,000 pair-cost increase.
  • Device formulary. The ANOC may not list the full formulary; call the hearing benefit vendor named in the EOC (typically TruHearing, Nations Hearing, UnitedHealthcare Hearing) and request the 2027 device list. Rechargeable Bluetooth-enabled models are often upgrade-tier with substantial out-of-pocket costs.
  • Refit and warranty. Check how many refits are covered per year and the warranty duration. Three-year warranties are standard but can be reduced to one or two years for 2027.
The hidden hearing-aid tip: if you need hearing aids soon and your 2026 plan has a $2,000 allowance but your 2027 ANOC shows that dropping to $1,500, consider purchasing the aids before December 31, 2026 so the 2026 allowance applies. Most hearing benefit vendors process the claim based on the date of fitting, not the date of order.

Step 4 — Verify the 2027 Flex Card Covered-Items List

The flex card — the pre-loaded debit card that funds OTC, grocery, and in some plans utility expenses — is the supplemental benefit that is most visibly changing for 2027. Two shifts matter.

First, the dollar amounts. Carriers are reducing quarterly allowances on many plans, and the "unlimited at participating stores" language that appeared in some 2024-2026 marketing has largely been retired. Confirm the specific 2027 quarterly or monthly dollar amount in the ANOC.

Second, point-of-sale enforcement is tightening. For 2027, the card will check your cart at the register and decline items the plan does not cover, rather than processing the charge and performing a clawback later. This is why so many 2026 enrollees discovered mid-year that items they used the card for were "not actually covered" — the plan was recovering those dollars via the explanation of benefits. The 2027 model declines them at the register. If a plan advertises a flex card, you are entitled to the 2027 covered-items list before enrolling; call member services or the card vendor and request it.

Grocery allowances tied to chronic conditions remain available on many SSBCI-eligible plans, but eligibility verification is stricter. If you believe you qualify (diabetes, cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, obesity, and several others), confirm that your medical record will support the chronic-condition flag the plan uses. If the plan cannot verify your chronic condition in its claims data, the grocery benefit will not be activated.

Step 5 — Compare Across the Three or Four Plans You Are Considering

Build a simple one-page comparison table before enrolling. The columns are the plans; the rows are the benefits you actually use. For most retirees that is:

  • Monthly premium and Part B giveback (if any)
  • In-network and (if PPO) combined out-of-pocket maximum
  • Primary care, specialist, and urgent care copays
  • Comprehensive dental maximum and major-services coinsurance
  • Hearing aid allowance per aid and device formulary vendor
  • Vision frame allowance and vendor
  • Flex card annual total and covered item category list
  • Over-the-counter quarterly allowance
  • Transportation, gym (SilverSneakers / Renew Active), and any chronic-condition benefits

Weight the rows by how much you actually use each benefit. A $3,000 dental maximum you will not use is less valuable than a $500 hearing aid allowance you will. One honest hour with the ANOCs for your current plan and the two or three alternatives you are considering is almost always enough to find a better 2027 fit, especially if your current plan is being materially trimmed.

Don't trust the Plan Finder star-rating alone. The CMS star rating is a composite that includes clinical quality, member satisfaction, and administrative performance. A 5-star plan can still have a weak dental or hearing benefit for 2027. Read the DVH rows yourself.

If Your Current Plan Is Being Trimmed, What Are Your Options?

Three categories of alternatives, in rough order of effort:

  1. Another Medicare Advantage plan in the same county with better 2027 DVH benefits. This is the most common move. The AEP is designed exactly for this, and the switch is handled through medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE. Coverage switches on January 1.
  2. Original Medicare plus a standalone Part D plan plus a Medigap policy. Original Medicare does not cover routine dental, vision, or hearing, so this path only makes sense if the DVH benefits are not the primary reason you have Medicare Advantage in the first place. If you make this switch after your initial Medigap enrollment window has closed, you may face medical underwriting unless your state offers a birthday rule or other guaranteed-issue window.
  3. Original Medicare plus a Part D plan plus a standalone dental, vision, and hearing policy. This is the most flexible but requires researching standalone DVH policies, which vary widely in quality. Delta Dental, Humana, Cigna, and MetLife all sell standalone Medicare DVH policies, with annual maximums ranging from $1,000 to $5,000 and premiums from $25 to $75 per month.

For most retirees who use their DVH benefits actively, switching to another MA plan with stronger 2027 DVH coverage is the simpler path. For retirees whose DVH use is low but whose medical access needs are high, the Original Medicare path may fit better. The right answer depends on how much the DVH benefits are actually worth to your household in a typical year.

The Five-Minute Verification Checklist

Short form, for returning readers:

  1. Pull up your ANOC and open to the supplemental benefits section.
  2. For dental, compare the 2026 and 2027 annual maximum, coinsurance tiers, and waiting periods.
  3. For vision, compare the frame allowance, vendor, and lens upgrade costs.
  4. For hearing, compare the per-aid allowance and request the 2027 device formulary from the hearing vendor.
  5. For the flex card, compare the quarterly/monthly dollar amount and request the 2027 covered-items list.
  6. If any benefit you actively use dropped, open medicare.gov's Plan Finder and compare two or three alternative 2027 plans on the same rows.
  7. Enroll in your chosen plan by December 7, 2026 for a January 1, 2027 coverage start.

Five minutes is enough for most people. The payoff — catching a $500 cut you did not know about, or spotting a plan with a stronger DVH package — is often worth far more than the time.

Where to Find Help

Three free sources are consistently reliable:

  • State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP). Every state operates a SHIP that provides free, unbiased counseling on Medicare decisions. Find your local office at shiphelp.org or 1-877-839-2675. SHIP counselors are not paid on commission and will not sell you a plan.
  • Medicare.gov Plan Finder. The federal government's own comparison tool. It is more accurate for 2027 than any carrier's marketing site.
  • 1-800-MEDICARE. Open 24/7 during AEP. Representatives can walk you through enrollment and benefits questions and are not commissioned.

Independent brokers can also help, but they are paid commissions by the carriers whose plans they sell, and the commission structure varies by plan. If you use a broker, ask explicitly which carriers they represent and request a comparison of plans across all carriers in your county, not only the ones that pay their highest commission.
