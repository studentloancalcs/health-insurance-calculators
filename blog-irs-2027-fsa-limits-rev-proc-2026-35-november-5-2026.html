IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-35 Sets the 2027 FSA Limits: $3,500 Health FSA, $700 Carryover, $350 Transit — What to Change in Your November 2026 Election

By HealthCalc Team

Published November 9, 2026

11 min read

The IRS released Revenue Procedure 2026-35 on November 5, 2026, finalizing the health and welfare benefit inflation adjustments for plan years beginning in 2027. The headline figures:

  • Health FSA salary reduction cap: $3,500 (up $100 from $3,400 in 2026).
  • Health FSA carryover maximum: $700 (up $20 from $680).
  • Qualified transportation fringe: $350 per month for mass transit/vanpool, and $350 per month for qualified parking (each up from $340).
  • Adoption credit and employer adoption assistance exclusion: $18,250, with the MAGI phase-out starting at $273,680 and complete at $313,680.

One figure that did not change: the dependent-care FSA limit stays at $7,500 (or $3,750 if married filing separately) because it is a statutory amount set by the 2025 reconciliation law, not an inflation-indexed cap. Rev. Proc. 2026-35 is silent on section 129 of the Code, which is the right outcome — there is nothing for the IRS to adjust annually under current law.

The timing matters. Open enrollment on HealthCare.gov opened November 1, and most employer open enrollment windows close between mid-November and early December. If you were working from projected 2027 figures last week, the final numbers are now out, the differences are small, and the only change you may need to make to your election is modest. Below is what each number means for a typical household's enrollment paperwork this month.

At a glance: Rev. Proc. 2026-35 is the IRS's annual catch-up on fringe-benefit inflation adjustments; it does not change HSA limits (those were set in Rev. Proc. 2026-24 in May 2026 at $4,500 self-only / $8,900 family). The 2027 Medicare Part D OOP cap ($2,400), maximum Part D deductible ($700), and average PDP premium (~$36) are governed by CMS and are also unchanged by this Rev. Proc.

Health FSA: The $3,500 Cap Means Roughly $25 More in Tax Savings

Electing the full $3,500 for a 2027 Health FSA versus the $3,400 you could have elected for 2026 is $100 of additional pre-tax contribution. For a household in the 22% federal bracket with 7.65% FICA, the additional federal and payroll tax savings on that $100 is $29.65. State tax savings add $2 to $10 depending on your state. For a household in the 24% federal bracket the additional federal/FICA savings is $31.65.

That is a small number, but the full tax savings on the entire $3,500 election is material: at 22% federal plus 7.65% FICA the federal/FICA savings on a maxed Health FSA is $1,038, and at 24% plus FICA it is $1,108. If you have predictable 2027 medical expenses (prescriptions, orthodontics, planned medical procedures, dependents' copays, a new pair of glasses), the Health FSA remains one of the highest-return pre-tax accounts available.

Who should elect the new $3,500 maximum?

The election should cover expenses you are reasonably certain you will incur in 2027, not expenses you hope to incur. The Health FSA is subject to the use-it-or-lose-it rule, modified by the $700 2027 carryover if your employer permits it, so the risk of over-electing is forfeiture. Over-electing by $200 in a 22% bracket costs you a net $141 (the $200 forfeiture minus the $59 you did save in federal and FICA tax on that $200). Under-electing in a year where you would have spent the money anyway costs the full tax shield.

How the $700 carryover changes the math

If your employer's plan allows carryover, up to $700 of unused 2027 Health FSA dollars can move into the 2028 plan year. That lowers the effective risk of over-election: $300 of over-election carries forward in full if under the carryover cap, and only the portion above $700 is forfeited. It does not eliminate the arithmetic, though — the carryover is available only if your employer elected it in the plan document, and employers with a grace period instead of carryover have the opposite trade-off.

Dependent-Care FSA: $7,500 Stays, but Check Your Plan Document

The dependent-care FSA cap was raised from $5,000 to $7,500 by the 2025 reconciliation law and is not indexed for inflation. The 2027 figure is $7,500 for most filers and $3,750 for married filing separately. Rev. Proc. 2026-35 does not touch it.

Where this gets tricky in 2026 OEP: many employer plan documents still cap dependent-care contributions at $5,000. The 2025 law raised the IRS maximum, but employers are not required to permit the full amount, and many plan documents were drafted before the increase and have not been amended. Before electing above $5,000, pull your Summary Plan Description or confirm with HR that the plan's dependent-care FSA cap was updated for the 2027 plan year.

Who the $7,500 cap primarily helps

Dual-earner households with two or more children in paid care. At median full-time daycare costs in a mid-cost metro (roughly $18,000 per year per child in 2026), the first $7,500 of care is now fully shielded if both parents meet the earned-income requirement. For one child, the $5,000-to-$7,500 range moves the FSA from "covers most of my qualifying expense" to "covers my full qualifying expense" because the dependent-care tax-credit limit also stops at $3,000 of expenses per child.

Tax-credit interaction: Dollars run through the dependent-care FSA are not eligible for the dependent-care tax credit on the same expenses. For a household with AGI above roughly $43,000, the FSA almost always beats the credit at the margin because the FSA excludes both federal income tax and FICA, while the credit refunds only federal income tax. Below that AGI the credit's 35% rate may edge out the FSA's combined 22% + 7.65% = 29.65% blended rate; run both before electing above $5,000.

Commuter Benefits: $350 Transit and $350 Parking

The qualified transportation fringe benefits for 2027 are $350 per month for mass transit and vanpooling, and $350 per month for qualified parking at or near the workplace or at a mass-transit terminal. These are separate caps, not a shared one — you can elect $350 of pre-tax transit and $350 of pre-tax parking in the same month, up to $700 per month in combined fringe benefits if you use both.

Transit commuter benefits are employer-provided and subject to your employer offering a qualified transportation plan. If yours does, the marginal effect of the $10/month increase from 2026 is small but real: $120 per year of additional shielded contribution, worth $34.75 to a 22% federal / 7.65% FICA filer and $37.98 to a 24% federal / 7.65% FICA filer.

For borderline transit commuters, the new $350/month cap also closes some of the gap between the pre-tax benefit and the full cost of an unlimited monthly pass in high-cost metros. An unlimited monthly MTA pass in New York is $132 in 2026; a WMATA SmarTrip Metrorail-plus-bus commuter in Washington D.C. typically spends $180-$250 per month. In both cases the $350 cap is well above the actual monthly spend, so the pre-tax shelter is effectively unlimited.

Adoption Credit and Employer Adoption Assistance: $18,250

The maximum adoption credit under section 23 for 2027 is $18,250 per child, with the MAGI phase-out beginning at $273,680 and complete at $313,680. The employer-provided adoption assistance exclusion under section 137 is also $18,250 with the same phase-out thresholds.

For families in the middle of a multi-year adoption process (foster-to-adopt, international adoption, agency adoption with a 12-to-24-month timeline), the 2026-to-2027 transition can shift which tax year carries the credit. The credit is claimed in the year the adoption is finalized for a U.S. child, and in the earlier of the year expenses were paid or the year of finalization for an international adoption. If your paperwork is tracking toward late 2026, there is a meaningful tax-year decision around whether to accelerate or defer the finalization for a $580 difference in the maximum credit — with the usual caveat that the credit becomes fully refundable for 2024 and later tax years only up to the lesser of the taxpayer's qualified adoption expenses or the maximum credit, and the credit's non-refundable portion may still interact with your other liabilities.

Five Changes to Your November 2026 Open-Enrollment Paperwork

1Raise your Health FSA election by up to $100. If you were going to contribute the 2026 maximum of $3,400 and expect similar 2027 medical spend, bump the 2027 election to $3,500. Ignore the increase only if you have predictable 2027 expenses below $3,400 anyway.

2Confirm your employer's dependent-care FSA cap is $7,500, not $5,000. Many plan documents still have the pre-2026 figure. If your plan still caps at $5,000, HR may be able to amend the plan document before the 2027 plan year begins; if not, the $5,000 cap binds for 2027 regardless of the IRS maximum.

3Check whether your 2026 Health FSA will carry $680 or less into 2027. This is your 2026 carryover, under the 2026 Rev. Proc. The 2027 carryover of $700 governs unused 2027 dollars moving into 2028. If you have a $900 balance in your 2026 FSA on December 31, 2026, only $680 carries over and $220 is forfeited unless your employer runs a grace period instead.

4Re-elect commuter benefits to $350/month if you had been capped at $340. This is a small change and automatic in some payroll systems; others require you to re-enter the figure during OEP.

5If you are enrolled in both an HSA and a Health FSA, confirm the FSA is Limited-Purpose or Post-Deductible. A general-purpose FSA disqualifies HSA contributions for the whole plan year, including contributions already made. The $3,500 cap applies to the Limited-Purpose FSA just as it does to the general one.

What the Rev. Proc. Does Not Change

Several figures are frequently confused with the FSA/fringe-benefit limits set in Rev. Proc. 2026-35; these are governed elsewhere and are unchanged by this release:

  • HSA contribution limits: $4,500 self-only / $8,900 family for 2027 (set by Rev. Proc. 2026-24, published May 2026). Catch-up remains $1,000 for age 55+.
  • HDHP minimum deductible: $1,700 self-only / $3,400 family for 2026; TBD for 2027 in Rev. Proc. 2026-24 (we will update once CMS and the IRS confirm the final figure for the 2027 HDHP definition).
  • HDHP annual out-of-pocket maximum: $8,500 self-only / $17,000 family for 2026; also addressed in Rev. Proc. 2026-24 for 2027.
  • Medicare Part D OOP cap: $2,400 for 2027 (up from $2,100 in 2026), set under the Inflation Reduction Act.
  • Medicare Part D maximum annual deductible: $700 for 2027, set by CMS.
  • ACA subsidy formula: Not addressed in a Rev. Proc. The pre-2021 premium contribution percentages and 400% FPL cliff continue to apply for 2027 unless Congress enacts an extension of the expired enhanced credits.
  • 401(k) and other retirement limits: Set in a separate IRS notice, typically released in early November each year. The 2027 figures are in Notice 2026-71 (released around the same time as Rev. Proc. 2026-35) and are not covered in this piece.

Three Mistakes to Avoid in Your 2027 FSA Election

Mistake 1: Treating the $100 Health FSA increase as reason to re-think the whole election

The $3,400-to-$3,500 move is a small inflation adjustment, not a policy change. If you had a well-calibrated 2026 election, nudge it up by $100 and move on. The bigger election-amount risk every year is mis-forecasting your actual qualified medical expenses by several hundred dollars in either direction, and no inflation adjustment changes that forecasting work.

Mistake 2: Assuming the $7,500 dependent-care cap is in your plan

Two years after the statutory increase, employer plan documents are still catching up unevenly. Check the SPD or ask HR in writing before electing above $5,000. If your plan caps at $5,000 and you contribute $6,500 through payroll anyway, the excess is treated as taxable wages, and your payroll system may or may not catch the issue before the W-2 goes out.

Mistake 3: Double-counting the Health FSA against the HSA

If you have an HDHP and an HSA, enrollment in a general-purpose Health FSA — whether yours or your spouse's — disqualifies you from HSA contributions for the entire plan year, including the January contribution you already made. The fix during OEP is to switch to a Limited-Purpose FSA (dental and vision only) or a Post-Deductible FSA (eligible after you hit the HDHP minimum deductible). Both are HSA-compatible and the $3,500 cap applies.
