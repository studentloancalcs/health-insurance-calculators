IRS Notice 2026-66 Sets the 2027 Retirement Limits: 401(k) $25,500, IRA $8,000, Catch-up $8,000 — How It Changes Your Paycheck Allocation Next Year

By HealthCalc Team

Published November 23, 2026

11 min read

The IRS released Notice 2026-66 on November 12, 2026, finalizing the 2027 cost-of-living adjustments for qualified retirement plans and IRAs. The headline figures:

  • 401(k), 403(b), 457(b), and federal Thrift Savings Plan elective deferral: $25,500 (up $1,000 from $24,500 in 2026).
  • IRA contribution limit (Traditional and Roth): $8,000 (up $500 from $7,500).
  • Age-50 catch-up (401(k) / 403(b) / 457(b)): $8,000 (up $500 from $7,500).
  • IRA age-50 catch-up: $1,100 (no change).
  • SECURE 2.0 super catch-up (ages 60–63) under section 414(v)(2)(E): remains in effect for 2027 at the greater of $11,250 or 150% of the regular catch-up.

The timing matters even for people who do not touch their 401(k) election during the year. Open enrollment on HealthCare.gov is now in week four, most employer open-enrollment windows are closing between mid-November and early December, and the 2027 numbers are the ones your January paycheck will run against. If you took the HSA and FSA companion Rev. Proc. 2026-35 figures into account last cycle, this is the matching piece on the retirement side.

At a glance: Notice 2026-66 is the IRS's annual cost-of-living adjustment for retirement plans under Code section 415(d). Its 401(k) limit, IRA limit, and catch-up amounts are separate from and in addition to the HSA limit ($4,500 self-only / $8,900 family for 2027), the Health FSA limit ($3,500), the dependent-care FSA limit ($7,500 statutory), and the Medicare Part D OOP cap ($2,400). An HSA-eligible worker can contribute to all of those in the same year up to each separate limit.

401(k): $25,500 Means Roughly $85 to $310 More in Federal Tax Savings

Electing the full $25,500 for a 2027 Traditional 401(k) versus the $24,500 you could have elected for 2026 is $1,000 of additional pre-tax contribution. For a household in the 22% federal bracket, the additional federal income tax shielded on that $1,000 is $220. State tax savings add $0 to $100 depending on your state. In the 24% bracket the additional federal savings is $240, and in the 32% bracket it is $320.

FICA does not apply to the incremental dollar the same way: for a worker above the 2027 Social Security wage base (currently projected at $184,500, though SSA will confirm separately), the incremental dollar avoids only the 1.45% Medicare rate. Below the wage base, the 7.65% combined FICA also does not shield a 401(k) contribution because the 401(k) deferral is not a Section 125 cafeteria-plan election. This is the single most-common mistake in paycheck arithmetic: a Traditional 401(k) saves federal and most state income taxes, but not FICA. A Health FSA or HSA (through a payroll cafeteria plan) saves FICA; a 401(k) does not.

Who should move to the new $25,500?

Anyone who was contributing at or near the 2026 maximum of $24,500 should raise the per-paycheck withholding percentage so the 2027 total reaches $25,500. On a 26-paycheck schedule that is $980.77 per paycheck, versus $942.31 on the old maximum. On 24 paychecks it is $1,062.50 versus $1,020.83. Most 401(k) platforms accept a percentage of gross pay rather than a dollar amount, so if you were at 15% of a $170,000 salary last year, that was $25,500 on the nose and you are already set; if you were at 15% of a $163,333 salary, that was $24,500, and you need either a bump to 15.6% or a plan-level override. Check whether your plan offers an auto-escalation feature that captures the IRS adjustment each year; several large recordkeepers do.

Age-50 catch-up: $8,000 means $33,500 total

The age-50 catch-up contribution for 2027 is $8,000, a $500 increase. Combined with the $25,500 base deferral, a worker age 50 or older (but not age 60 to 63) can contribute up to $33,500 in 2027. If you were at the 2026 age-50 maximum of $32,000, you have $1,500 of new contribution room. The catch-up applies by payroll deduction over the year; set it in the recordkeeper's "catch-up" field, not by overriding the regular deferral field.

Super catch-up (ages 60–63): stays in effect for 2027

SECURE 2.0 section 109 added a higher catch-up for workers who have reached age 60 but have not yet turned 64 by the end of the calendar year. For 2027 the super catch-up remains at the greater of $11,250 or 150% of the regular age-50 catch-up, so with the regular catch-up now at $8,000, the 150% figure is $12,000, and the super catch-up for 2027 is $12,000. Combined with the $25,500 deferral, a worker in that five-year window can contribute up to $37,500 in 2027.

Roth catch-up rule: Under final Treasury/IRS regulations issued in September 2025 implementing SECURE 2.0 section 603, catch-up contributions by employees whose FICA wages from the sponsoring employer exceeded $145,000 in the prior calendar year (indexed) must be made on a Roth (after-tax) basis beginning in 2026. For 2027, that threshold is the first full-year cycle. If your 2026 FICA wages from the same employer exceeded the indexed threshold, your 2027 age-50 catch-up and, if applicable, super catch-up must go to the Roth source within the 401(k). This does not change the dollar amount of the catch-up; it changes the tax-treatment column.

IRA: $8,000 for Everyone, $9,100 at Age 50 and Up

The 2027 Traditional and Roth IRA contribution limit rises $500 to $8,000. The age-50 catch-up stays at $1,100 (SECURE 2.0 indexed this catch-up starting 2024, but the small-dollar step means the 2027 figure remains at $1,100). Total at age 50 and up: $9,100.

IRA contributions are made to an individual account outside employer payroll. You open and fund the IRA directly at a custodian such as Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard, Betterment, or a bank; you have until April 15, 2028 to make a 2027 contribution. That calendar is helpful on both sides: it means you can wait to see your 2027 income before deciding Traditional vs. Roth, and it means any lump-sum bonus, refund, or year-end deposit can still land as a 2027 contribution.

Roth IRA phase-out: where the direct contribution gates close

Roth IRA eligibility phases out by modified adjusted gross income. The 2027 ranges under Notice 2026-66:

Filing status Phase-out begins (MAGI) Phase-out complete (MAGI)
Single, head of household $164,000 $179,000
Married filing jointly $258,000 $268,000
Married filing separately (living with spouse) $0 $10,000

Below the start of the phase-out, you can contribute the full $8,000 (or $9,100 at age 50+). Within the phase-out, the limit scales linearly down to zero at the top. Above the top, you cannot contribute directly to a Roth IRA. A backdoor Roth conversion from a Traditional IRA may still be available, but it is cleanest if you have no pre-tax IRA balance anywhere (SEP-IRAs, SIMPLE IRAs, and rollover IRAs all count against the pro-rata rule).

Traditional IRA deduction: where the pre-tax door narrows

A Traditional IRA contribution is always allowed up to $8,000 (plus the $1,100 catch-up), but the deduction phases out by MAGI and depends on whether you (and your spouse) are covered by a workplace retirement plan. The 2027 deduction phase-outs from Notice 2026-66:

Filing status / coverage Phase-out begins (MAGI) Phase-out complete (MAGI)
Single, covered by workplace plan $83,000 $103,000
MFJ, filer covered by workplace plan $132,000 $152,000
MFJ, filer not covered but spouse is $248,000 $258,000

If you are above the deduction phase-out but still contribute a Traditional IRA, the contribution is non-deductible, and you must file Form 8606 to establish basis. Non-deductible Traditional contributions are the first leg of the classic backdoor-Roth conversion if you have no other pre-tax IRA balance.

How the 2027 401(k) and IRA Numbers Interact with Your HSA

The 401(k) and HSA do not share a limit. Each stands alone: $25,500 to the 401(k) plus $4,500 self-only (or $8,900 family) to the HSA, both in the same year, if you are HSA-eligible (enrolled in a qualifying HDHP, not enrolled in Medicare, not another person's tax dependent). The IRA is also separate. A dual-earner household where one spouse has HDHP family coverage can in 2027 contribute:

  • Up to $25,500 each in 401(k) deferrals = $51,000 pre-tax to employer plans.
  • Up to $8,900 to one HSA (family coverage limit), split between the spouses by any ratio they agree on.
  • Up to $8,000 each to IRAs = $16,000 (Traditional or Roth, subject to the MAGI phase-outs above).
  • Up to $3,500 each to Health FSAs (though pairing a general-purpose Health FSA with an HSA disqualifies the HSA for the whole plan year; only Limited-Purpose or Post-Deductible FSAs are HSA-compatible).

That is a theoretical maximum of $79,400 in tax-advantaged employee contributions for a dual-earner, age-under-50 household in 2027. Most households cannot reach that ceiling because of cash flow; the question is the order.

Standard priority order for 2027

1Capture the employer 401(k) match in full. A typical 50% match on the first 6% of pay is an immediate 50% return on contribution and is the highest-return dollar in the stack. Do this first regardless of any other goal.

2Max the HSA ($4,500 self-only / $8,900 family in 2027). HSA contributions avoid federal and most state income tax, plus FICA (when made through payroll). They grow tax-free and are tax-free on withdrawal for qualified medical expenses at any age. No other account has all three features. See our HSA vs FSA Calculator for your household-specific numbers.

3Return to the 401(k) up to the $25,500 limit. Traditional saves federal and state income tax at today's marginal rate; Roth takes the hit today and withdraws tax-free. For most mid-career households, a mix tilted toward Traditional is the right default; high earners should consider the Roth-leaning tilt only after the match and HSA are maxed.

4Max the IRA ($8,000 or $9,100 at age 50+). Use the Roth form if you are under the phase-out and want tax-free growth; use the Traditional form if you are above the Roth phase-out but below the deduction phase-out. Backdoor Roth is available if both phase-outs are closed and you have no other pre-tax IRA balance.

5Health FSA at the 2027 $3,500 limit, chosen as Limited-Purpose or Post-Deductible if you are an HSA holder. Pre-tax at FICA plus federal and state income tax, with use-it-or-lose-it moderated by the 2027 $700 carryover.

6Taxable brokerage, I-Bonds, or 529 (depending on your goals). Any contribution beyond the above goes into taxable accounts or specialized tax-preferred vehicles.

Three Mistakes to Avoid in Your 2027 Retirement Allocation

Mistake 1: Treating the 401(k) as FICA-advantaged

Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce federal and most state income tax but do not reduce FICA. If you are optimizing paycheck take-home and planning between an incremental HSA dollar (cafeteria plan, FICA-exempt) and an incremental 401(k) dollar (not FICA-exempt), the HSA has a 7.65% head start before any other factor. For a single high-income earner above the Social Security wage base, the margin narrows to the Medicare rate only (1.45%), but the ordering still favors HSA first.

Mistake 2: Skipping the HSA to prioritize the IRA

The IRA has longer, more flexible investment menus than most HSAs and is often recommended first for savers who dislike HSA custodian fees. That reasoning is right for the investment-option question but wrong for the tax-shelter question: the HSA is the only account that avoids all three of federal income tax, FICA, and tax on qualified-medical withdrawal. A sub-optimal HSA custodian can be remediated by consolidation (most HSAs permit transfers out), but the HSA's tax treatment cannot be recreated in any other account. Fund the HSA in the custodian your employer offers, and then transfer the balance out annually to a better custodian if the first one is sub-par.

Mistake 3: Treating catch-ups as automatic

The 401(k) age-50 catch-up (and the ages-60–63 super catch-up) are not automatic. You must elect them in your recordkeeper's catch-up field or raise your payroll deduction percentage until it generates the higher total. Some payroll systems cut you off at the base $25,500 limit if you have not activated the catch-up election. Check your January 2027 paystub to confirm the base and catch-up are coming out at the intended ratio.

What the Notice Does Not Change

  • HSA contribution limits: $4,500 self-only / $8,900 family for 2027, catch-up $1,000 for age 55+. Set by Rev. Proc. 2026-24 back in May 2026.
  • Health FSA limit: $3,500 for 2027, carryover $700. Set by Rev. Proc. 2026-35 in November 2026.
  • Dependent-care FSA: $7,500 for 2027 (statutory, not indexed).
  • Medicare Part D OOP cap: $2,400 for 2027, up from $2,100 in 2026.
  • ACA premium tax credit formula: Not addressed in Notice 2026-66. The pre-2021 premium contribution percentages and the 400% FPL cliff continue to apply for 2027 unless Congress enacts an extension of the expired enhanced credits.
  • Social Security taxable wage base for 2027: Set by the Social Security Administration, released separately in October 2026. Projected around $184,500. The wage base affects the FICA calculation on high earners but does not change the 401(k) or IRA limit.
